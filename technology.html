A cell platform · Lithium-sulfur · Solid-state

A cell platform,
not a chemistry experiment.

Lithium-sulfur, made manufacturable. A proprietary additive activates the sulfur cathode, suppresses the polysulfide shuttle, and unlocks a stable two-plateau discharge profile — on the same roll-to-roll equipment incumbent chemistries already use.

01 · The chemistry

An additive that activates sulfur.

Sulfur is the cathode the industry has wanted for thirty years. It is light, abundant, and a refining byproduct sourced overwhelmingly inside US-allied geographies. The reason it has not shipped at scale is a single failure mode: the polysulfide shuttle. Dissolved intermediates ferry between electrodes during cycling, irreversibly degrading capacity within tens of cycles.

Caldera's additive, exclusively licensed from Johns Hopkins APL, suppresses that shuttle. The cathode stays active, the cell stays stable, and the manufacturing process changes by exactly one input: a swap at the cathode-coating step.
