Act I · The wall

The ceiling we have already hit.

For two decades, lithium-ion has improved at the margins. The cells in the world's best EV today are not meaningfully more energy-dense than the cells in a 2015 laptop. The chemistry is mature; the curve is flat. Programs that need step-change endurance — high-altitude, long-range — have been waiting for the next platform.

Act II · The chemistry

A proprietary additive that unlocks sulfur.

Sulfur is the most abundant battery-grade material on Earth and one of the cheapest. It has long been the obvious successor to manganese and cobalt — and just as long, it has resisted commercialization. Caldera Voltaics has developed a proprietary additive that unlocks the electrochemical potential of sulfur, validated at a National Laboratory across 17 patented proprietary technologies.

Act III · The mission

Built for the platforms the next decade needs.

Caldera operates from Washington, DC and Laurel, Maryland — co-located with the laboratories and the program offices that will field this technology first. Defense, aerospace, and long-haul mobility — in that order.
